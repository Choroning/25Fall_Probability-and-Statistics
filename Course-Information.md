## 2. Schedule and Class Format

- **Credits (Hours):** 3 (3)
- **Intended For:** Second-year students (major required)
- **Language of Instruction:** English
- **Class Times:** Monday period 4 (12:00 ~ 13:00), Wednesday periods 5-6 (13:00 ~ 14:50)
- **Classroom:** Room 7-315 (Mon, Wed)
- **Class Format:** Lectures, discussions, practice, individual activities, quizzes, and Q&A.
- **Course Description:** An elementary introduction to probability and statistics for computer scientists. Learning the concepts of probability and statistics, a key part of big data and artificial intelligence, is essential in preparing for the fourth industrial revolution, and the course covers the theory of statistical techniques for dealing with data produced in various places. Students learn the concepts of statistics from A to Z, from probability to data analysis, and implement the theory learned in the lectures directly in R (a statistical programming language) to apply their theoretical knowledge to real applications.
- **Lectures:** Lectures use the slides uploaded on Blackboard and give opportunities to think about the academic issues raised during the lecture through Q&A. Students are highly recommended to review the relevant areas in advance according to the course schedule.
- **Practice:** Knowledge of a theory that one fully understands but cannot implement is useless, so the theory learned in class is implemented through R programming.
- **Prerequisites:** The course is for second-year students and requires high school level mathematics. Any programming language (C, C++, C#, Java, Python, R, SAS, etc.) is helpful.
- **Attendance:** Attendance is checked.
- **Exam Supervision:** Exams are not held unsupervised.
- **Study Objectives:** Students who complete the course are expected to learn the following.
  1. Concepts and applications of probability and statistics
  2. Fundamentals of probability
  3. Fundamentals of statistics
  4. Statistical estimation and modeling
  5. Hypothesis testing
  6. Fundamentals of R programming

## 4. Course Policies

- Assignment answers are written in Korean or English. Handwritten assignments must be written neatly enough for the grader to read, simple calculation errors in answers that clearly show the whole process are not penalized, and final results in fraction form need not be reduced.
- For R assignments, the report (.docx) and the entire R code written (.R) are compressed under a name with the assignment number, such as `HW2_YourID_Yourname.zip`, and submitted to Blackboard, and the R code must run cleanly when the teaching assistant runs it.
- Students may consult with friends, but copying R code is strictly prohibited.
- In all processes of taking the course (attending lectures, writing and submitting assignments, exams, etc.), students must participate in learning with honesty and passion according to the purpose and educational philosophy of the university, devote themselves to creative learning based on moral sensitivity, faithfully carry out what they have promised the faculty and fellow students, and respect the character of the faculty and other students and observe basic manners.
- Credits obtained through dishonest means such as plagiarism, reuse of assignments from other classes, or cheating on exams are revoked even after they have been granted, and disciplinary action such as suspension, withdrawal, or expulsion may follow depending on the circumstances.
- The university operates a plagiarism inspection system (Blackboard) to raise awareness of learning ethics and to encourage creativity, and students must check in advance whether their submissions constitute plagiarism.
- Students with questions about support services for students with disabilities can consult Prof. Minseok Seo to find the best way to take the course for their type of disability.
