| [HW01_Probability-and-Random-Variables](HW01_Probability-and-Random-Variables/) | Assignment 1, given in week 7. It consists of 14 problems solved by hand without R code, on unknown constants in a probability table, conditional probability, independent events, Bayes' theorem, the probability mass function, expectation, and variance of the discrete distribution that fits each situation, the distribution of a maximum and linear transformations, the relation between $E[X^2]$ and the variance, the joint cumulative distribution function and independence from a joint probability mass function, and a mind map of Lectures 2-5. It connects to Lecture 2 (Probability Theory), Lecture 3 (Random Variables and Probability Distributions), Lecture 4 (Discrete Random Variables), and Lecture 5 (Continuous Random Variables and the Normal Distribution) | [Assignment handout](HW01_Probability-and-Random-Variables/Original/Assignment.md), [Assignment explanation](HW01_Probability-and-Random-Variables/Assignment-Explanation.md) |
| [HW02_One-Sample-Z-Test](HW02_One-Sample-Z-Test/) | Assignment 2. Analyzing clinical data of 8,921 subjects with R, it tests whether the systolic blood pressure of the whole sample differs from the NIH distribution for normal people (Q1) and whether that of COVID19 patients is higher than the national statistics (Q2), compares tests of male heart rate with alternative hypotheses in three directions at the 5% and 10% significance levels (Q3, Q4), and compares resting oxygen saturation with Korean health checkup statistics (Q5). It connects to Lecture 6 (Hypothesis Testing) | [Assignment handout](HW02_One-Sample-Z-Test/Original/Assignment.md), [Assignment explanation](HW02_One-Sample-Z-Test/Assignment-Explanation.md), [Code walkthrough](HW02_One-Sample-Z-Test/Code-Walkthrough.md), [R code](HW02_One-Sample-Z-Test/Solution/) |
| [HW03_Nonparametric-Tests-and-Multiple-Testing](HW03_Nonparametric-Tests-and-Multiple-Testing/) | Assignment 3, given in week 14. It compares the COVID-19 case counts of two occupation groups with a parametric and a nonparametric method and chooses the preferable test (Q1, Q2), tests with the Seo statistic, the absolute difference of medians, and discusses its limitations (Q3, Q4), finds among 1,274 genes those that differ between the cancer and healthy groups (Q5) and those related to height (Q6), treats the multiple testing problem and its correction (Q7), and describes the use of the results (Q8). It connects to Lecture 6 (Hypothesis Testing), Lecture 7 (Statistical Analysis), and Lecture 8 (Nonparametric Approaches) | [Assignment handout](HW03_Nonparametric-Tests-and-Multiple-Testing/Original/Assignment.md), [Assignment explanation](HW03_Nonparametric-Tests-and-Multiple-Testing/Assignment-Explanation.md), [Code walkthrough](HW03_Nonparametric-Tests-and-Multiple-Testing/Code-Walkthrough.md), [R code](HW03_Nonparametric-Tests-and-Multiple-Testing/Solution/) |
| [Lab01_Introduction-to-R-Programming](Lab01_Introduction-to-R-Programming/) | Code from the R practice class of week 2 (9/8). It covers creating vectors and `class()`, the structure and summary of the iris data, `rep()` and `seq()`, plotting normal random numbers made with `rnorm()`, data frames and factors, and a ggplot2 box plot, and connects to Lecture 1 (Introduction to R Programming) | [Lab_R-Basics.R](Lab01_Introduction-to-R-Programming/Lab_R-Basics.R) |
